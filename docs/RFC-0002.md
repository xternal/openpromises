## 1. Summary

Pavel asked on 17 October 2026: "can we do something like a "educated calculation of a cost of a promise"? in terms of money, people, time, changes, etc -- and always add сравнения, например, "за эти деньги можно построить 10 поликлиник или проложить 150км дорог" (or this should be implemented in OpenPromises SDK first?)". Later he asked to "publish the cost comparison proposal too".

This RFC proposes the answer to his last question first: **yes, in the engine.** All three sites need it, and it touches the card format, a check, a computation, a component and the open data, which decision 24 puts in the engine.

It proposes five things a card can show:

1. **Money:** the cost, as today, and, when no one else costed the promise, our own estimate, clearly labelled.
2. **People:** the staff a promise needs, and the people it reaches.
3. **Time:** how long delivery usually takes, against the promise's own deadline.
4. **Changes:** the steps that delivery needs (a law, a budget line, a procurement), and which are done.
5. **Comparisons:** what the cost equals, in units the site chooses once for every card.

Nothing here is decided. §9 lists the questions for Pavel.

## 2. Why

A figure such as "£2.4bn a year" means little to most readers. Set beside "about £35 a person a year" or "the running cost of 8 to 12 polyclinics", it becomes something people can weigh. Today the engine shows a cost range with its source, and nothing more. Each site would otherwise build its own comparisons, and they would drift, as the three card formats did before RFC-0001.

Comparisons are also where a tracker can most easily slip from showing facts to making an argument. "Could build 10 polyclinics instead" tells the reader what the money *should* have bought. The rules in §5 exist to stop that, so the same promise gets the same treatment whichever party made it (RFC-0001 principle 5).

## 4. What a card would show

### 4.1 Money

Today a version's `parameters` holds `cost` (per `money.period`) and `capital_cost` (one-off). `cost.by.kind` is `official`, `party` or `independent`, and `money.qualities` already includes `modelled`.

New: **our own estimate**, for when no official body, party or independent source has costed the promise.

- `cost.by.kind: editorial`, a new kind, with `name` set to the site ("LedgerGov.UK"). This is kept apart from `independent`, because a site is not independent of its own estimate, and readers should see that.
- `quality: modelled`.
- A required `method` (a language map): the inputs multiplied by unit costs, each input with its source, so a reader can redo the sum.
- A minimum width: proposed at ±25% around the central figure at least. A model is less certain than a costing, and the range should say so.
- Labelled "Our estimate" (ru "Наша оценка") everywhere it is shown, through the messages, as decision 23 does for verification.
- It never replaces an official, party or independent figure. When one exists, the card uses that one, and our estimate can sit beside it as a second view only if §9 Q3 allows it.

### 4.2 People

Optional, under a new `estimate` object in `parameters`:

- `estimate.people.staff`: the staff a promise needs, as a full-time-equivalent range, with a unit per language ("teachers", "учителей") and sources.
- `estimate.people.reached`: the people it affects, as a range, with sources.

Comparisons for people use the same basket as money (§4.5): a share of the current workforce, or so many years of training output, when the site supplies those figures.

### 4.3 Time

Optional: `estimate.time.lead`, the months delivery is expected to take, as a range, with a `basis`:

- `official_plan`: from a plan or timetable, with its source.
- `reference_class`: worked out by the engine from the site's own cards (below).

It is shown against the promise's own deadline: "Usually takes 18–30 months; this promise allows 14."

The engine can work out a reference class from the site's own ledger: the time from `promised` to a finished status for cards in the same area. Two cautions:

- It is shown only when there are enough comparable cards: proposed at least 10.
- Taking only finished cards would understate the time, because the slow ones haven't finished yet. The words therefore say what the figure covers, for example: "Of 14 similar promises, 9 are finished; those took a median of 22 months." Doing better than that (survival analysis) can wait until a site asks for it.

### 4.4 Changes: the delivery path

Optional: `estimate.path`, the ordered steps delivery needs. Each step has:

- a step type: `primary_legislation`, `secondary_legislation`, `budget_line`, `procurement`, `planning_consent`, `new_body` or `recruitment`;
- an optional expected-by date;
- once it happens, the timeline event that met it.

A step type is listed in the configuration with its labels, as the status ladder is (RFC-0001 decision 6), so each country keeps its own words. The card can then show "3 of 6 steps done". A step is met only by an event with evidence (principle 2), so the path never moves on our say-so.

### 4.5 Comparisons

Comparisons are **worked out, never stored on the card**. They come from:

- `compare(cost, basket, options)` in `@openpromises/core`;
- a component in `@openpromises/react`;
- a `comparisons` field in the open data, in JSON and in CSV columns at the end;
- **not** the feeds: a change of basket would otherwise send old entries to followers again.

The site supplies the **basket** in its configuration, for example in `comparisons.yaml`. Each unit has:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `id` | A stable name, such as `polyclinic_running` |
| `label` | Per language, with plural forms ("polyclinic", "polyclinics"; "поликлиника", "поликлиники", "поликлиник") |
| `cost` | A unit cost range: low, central, high |
| `recurring` or `one_off` | Whether it is a yearly cost or a one-off |
| `price_year` | The year its prices are in |
| `source` | URL, publisher, vintage (the date the figure was published) and quality |

The order on the card:

1. per person, and per household (population and households are set in the configuration, with their year and source);
2. a share of a total the site supplies, such as a year's public spending;
3. the physical units, in the basket's order.

Arithmetic, range in and range out:

- **Count:** the cost range divided by the unit cost range, as [low ÷ high unit cost, central ÷ central, high ÷ low unit cost]. The uncertainties widen honestly.
- **Rounding:** to two significant figures, with "about" when rounded.

A basket is part of what a site publishes. A change to it changes every card at once, the same way for every party, and the site's changelog says when and why.

## 5. Rules

### 5.1 One basket for every card

A site has one fixed basket, shown in the same order on every card. It is never chosen card by card. Choosing per card is where bias would come in: polyclinics for one party's promise, fighter jets for another's. This is RFC-0001 principle 5, one standard for everyone, and the conformance test can prove it, because the comparison never depends on the card's party.

### 5.2 Neutral words

- en: "Equal to the cost of 8–12 polyclinics a year"
- ru: "Столько же стоит: 8–12 поликлиник в год"

The words never say "could build", "instead", "можно было бы" or "вместо". Those words suggest what the money should have bought, which is a judgement, and RussiaLedger.com's wording lint already rejects judgement words.

Pavel's own example ("за эти деньги можно построить…") would therefore be reworded. That is open question Q2: he may prefer his wording, and if he does, the reason for the neutral words should be weighed against it plainly.

### 5.3 Like with like

- A yearly cost compares with recurring units.
- A one-off cost compares with one-off units.
- The two cross only over a stated period: the promise's own period, labelled "Over 5 years, equal to …".

### 5.4 Every number has its source

- Every unit cost is an observation with a source, a vintage and a quality.
- The price year is shown.
- A comparison's quality is the worst quality among its inputs.
- A cost without a range gets no comparison.
- Prices in different years are not silently mixed: see Q8.

### 5.5 RussiaLedger.com

- A comparison never suggests knowledge of a classified part of spending. When `funding_verifiable` is `false`, the comparison says so beside it.
- Occupied regions (its D5) stay outside per-resident figures.

### 5.6 Machines draft, people decide

An estimate drafted by a model is a draft until the site's editorial rules publish it (principle 7). The model uses the site's own provider and key; the engine holds none (decision 26).

### 5.7 Accessible

Comparisons are plain text and a table. No chart is needed to read them.

## 6. Format sketch

For discussion, not final:

```yaml
versions:
  - parameters:
      cost:
        range: [1.8, 2.4, 3.2]
        quality: modelled
        by: { kind: editorial, name: LedgerGov.UK }
        method: { en: "6,500 teachers × £37k–£49k a year, employer cost (DfE STRB 2026)" }
        sources: [...]
      estimate:
        people:
          staff: { range: [6000, 6500, 7000], unit: { en: teachers }, sources: [...] }
        time:
          lead: { months: [18, 24, 36], basis: reference_class }
        path:
          - { step: budget_line, by: "2027-03-31" }
          - { step: recruitment }
```

Every new field is optional, so no valid card becomes invalid (decision 13). They would be late fields (FORMAT.md §9): they can be filled in once on a published version without a correction. `by.kind: editorial` is a new value for an existing field, so it adds nothing a site must change.

## 7. Engine work, if accepted

| Package | Work |
|---|---|
| `core` | Schemas for `estimate` and `by.kind: editorial`. Checks: the method is required, plus the minimum width, the path and the units. `compare()`, and the reference-class figure. |
| `publish` | Words for every language (messages), Markdown, the open data (`comparisons`, CSV columns at the end) and llms-full.txt. |
| `react` | A `Comparisons` component (text and a table), and the estimate in `PromiseCard`. |
| `files`, `cli` | Read `comparisons.yaml`, and check it: every unit has a source, a price year and labels in every language. |
| Docs | FORMAT.md, PUBLISHING.md, and a guide to building a fair basket. |

## 8. Risks

- **A biased basket.** A fixed basket stops bias from card to card, but not a biased basket. Mitigations: publish the basket, its sources and its history, and use the same rules for every party.
- **False precision.** Ranges, two significant figures and "about" keep it honest. Our estimates have a minimum width.
- **Stale unit costs.** Every vintage is shown. A check could warn when a vintage is older than a limit the site sets.
- **A misleading reference class.** Only finished cards would understate the time, so the words say what the figure covers (§4.3), and nothing is shown below the threshold.
- **Readers taking our estimate for an official one.** "Our estimate" is on every output, its `by.kind` is its own, and an official figure always comes first.

## 9. Open questions for the owner

| # | Question | Recommended | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | A fixed basket per site, or units chosen per card? | **Fixed per site** | Choosing per card is where bias would come in (§5.1). |
| Q2 | Neutral words ("Equal to the cost of …", "Столько же стоит: …") or "за эти деньги можно построить…"? | **Neutral** | "Could build" and "instead" suggest what the money should buy, and RussiaLedger.com's lint rejects judgement words. Pavel's wording is his to keep if he prefers it. |
| Q3 | Allow our own estimates (`by.kind: editorial`, `modelled`, at least ±25%)? And beside an official figure, or only when there is none? | **Yes, only when no one else costed it** | It fills gaps without competing with official figures. |
| Q4 | What the first release includes | **Money, comparisons and people**; time and path as optional fields; the reference class once n ≥ 10 | Money and comparisons are what readers asked for. Time and path need more data to be useful. |
| Q5 | Where the basket lives | **The site's configuration, with a source for each unit** | The site owns its context; the engine owns the rules. |
| Q6 | Comparisons in the open data and the feeds? | **Open data yes, feeds no** | A basket change would otherwise resend old feed entries. |
| Q7 | How many units a basket needs, and how many a card shows | **At least 3; the card shows 3, and a table lists them all** | Enough for context without crowding the card. |
| Q8 | Prices from different years | **Show each price year, and adjust only with a deflator the site supplies and names** | The engine makes no network calls, so a site brings its own index, with its source. |
| Q9 | Delivery-path step types | **Listed in the configuration, with labels, like the status ladder** | Each country's law-making differs. |
